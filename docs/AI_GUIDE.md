# Building with Lunaris: a guide for AI agents

You operate a deterministic game engine through tools. Everything below is also reachable from the `guide` tool, section by section (`guide {"topic": "behaviour"}`).

## The loop

1. `describe` (what is here), `schema` (every component and its fields), `files` (the textures, models and scenes in the workspace), `tools/list` (every tool and its arguments).
2. Build: `define_component`, `spawn`, `define_system`, `set_resource`, `save_scene`.
3. `check` lists mistakes that would otherwise be a blank picture or a value stuck at 0. Fix what it lists.
4. Look: `render` (an image plus which entities are visible), `minimap` (a text map), `pick`, `raycast`, `nearby`, `contacts`.
5. Run: `step`, then compare `hash` (same scene + seed + commands = same hash). Try things safely with `snapshot` / `restore`; every edit is undoable with `undo`.
6. Prove: `record_start` ... `record_stop` ... `replay` re-runs the whole session from the recording and checks the final hash.

Errors are written for you: a stable `code`, a `path` into your arguments and a `hint`. Read the hint, fix, retry.

## The world

- An **entity** is a number (its `uid`) plus **components**: plain data with named fields (`Transform.pos`, `Velocity.lin`). Define your own with `define_component` (types `f32 f64 i32 u32 i64 u64 bool str uid`, optional fixed array `count`, defaults).
- Standard components: `Name`, `Transform` (pos, rot quaternion x y z w, scale), `Velocity`, `Parent` + `LocalTransform` (attach to another entity), `Camera` (perspective, or `ortho` with `ortho_height`), `MeshRenderer` (`mesh`: cube, sphere, plane, cylinder, quad, or a `.glb` / `.gltf` path; `emissive` 0..1 makes it glow in the dark; `metallic`, `roughness`, `texture`, `uv_scale`, `bump`, `cast_shadow`, and a colour alpha below 1 for glass: the looks of the GPU renderer), `Animator` (plays a skinned model's clip; setting `clip` to another name crossfades over `fade` seconds, 0.25 by default), `Environment` (sky, clouds, fog, bloom, shadows, exposure, depth of field `dof` + `focus` + `focus_range` for the whole picture), `Sprite` (size, colour, `texture`, sprite sheet `sheet` + `frame`), `Tilemap` (a whole 2D level from a tileset), `Text` (HUD, with `{resource}` placeholders), `Light` (`kind` 0 sun, 1 point, 2 spot with `angle` and `softness`), `Emitter` (throws short-lived particles: fountains, fire, fireworks, with `burst` for one-off explosions), `Collider` (`shape` 0 sphere, 1 box, 2 capsule; a box with a turned `Transform.rot` is an oriented box) + `RigidBody` (physics) + `AngularVelocity` (makes the body turn: balls roll, boxes tip and topple; `docs/PHYSICS.md`), `CharacterBody` (a walking, jumping character: set `walk` [x, z speed] and `jump` from the input in a graph system, read `grounded`; its `Transform` is its feet; see `examples/walker.lunaris.json`), `Sound` (how an event name sounds), `SoundFile` (an event name that plays a WAV file, placed where the emitter was), `AudioSource` (a looping sound on an entity: a campfire, a radio), `AcousticMaterial` (how a collider absorbs and passes sound), `Terrain` (ground from a 16-bit greyscale heightmap PNG, drawn and stood on: `heightmap`, `size` in metres, `height` of white over black; `terrain_height {x, z}` asks the height of the ground, `scatter` puts forests and rocks on it, `reload_terrain` re-reads changed pictures; agents and `path` walk over it; `docs/TERRAIN.md`, `examples/terrain.lunaris.json`), `TerrainLayers` (beside a `Terrain`: up to four materials, one line each in `layers` as `NAME [TEXTURE] [uv_scale=N] [bump=D] [tint=R,G,B]` with a built-in material or a PNG as the texture, blended by the red, green, blue and alpha of a `splat` PNG laid over the ground like the heightmap, row 0 north; set `Terrain.color` to [1,1,1,1]; `terrain_layer {x, z}` says which layer is under a point, `scatter` takes `layer` and `layer_min`; Tellus's `splat.png` loads as it is; `docs/TERRAIN.md`, "Layers"), `MeshCollider` (static level geometry from a `.glb` / `.gltf`: put it beside the `MeshRenderer` that shows the same file and what you see is what bodies roll on, characters and agents walk on and rays hit; `friction`, `restitution`; a `RigidBody` does not go on it; `check` says what is wrong with the model; `examples/mesh_level.lunaris.json`), `ConvexCollider` (a dynamic or static convex solid with the shape of a `.glb` model's hull, at most 64 corners, mass spread over its volume: rocks, crates, wedges; `docs/PHYSICS.md`), `InputMap` (named input actions: `action.NAME`, `.pressed`, `.released` resources), `StateMachine` (states, transitions and actions in text; drives the `Animator`; for animation and AI), `Tween` (animate any number field with easing), `CameraRig` (follow, orbit, shake), `Sensor` (who is near, in view, in sight): all in `docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, `Joint` (a rope, rod or spring from a body to another body or a point: `kind` 0/1/2, `length`, `stiffness`, `damping`, `anchor`, `width` to draw it; `kind` 3 is a ball-and-socket and 4 a hinge between bodies that turn, with `anchor` the pivot in world coordinates, a hinge's `axis` and angle `limit`: doors, chains of capsules, ragdoll limbs; `docs/PHYSICS.md`, `examples/dominoes.lunaris.json`), `PhysicsSettings`, `CollisionLayer` (`layer` 0 to 31 and a `mask` of the layers it collides with, bit n = layer n; two entities touch only if each one's mask has the other's layer; it applies to contacts of every shape, triggers, characters, agents, sensors' sight and `raycast` (which takes its own `mask` or `layers`); an entity without it is layer 0 with a full mask; `docs/PHYSICS.md`), `Vehicle` (a raycast car on a chassis that has `Collider` + `RigidBody` + `Velocity` + `AngularVelocity`: `wheels` is text, one wheel a line `fl: -0.8 -0.15 -1.3 steer=1 brake=1` with mount point x y z, `radius`, `rest`, `travel`, `spring`, `damper`, `drive`, `grip`, `entity` ...; the chassis's -z is forward; a graph system sets `throttle` -1..1, `steer` -1..1, `brake`, `handbrake` from an `InputMap`; the engine writes `speed`, `steering`, `on_ground`, per-wheel `compression`, `spin`, `slip`; `render debug ["wheels"]` draws it; `docs/PHYSICS.md`, `examples/driving.lunaris.json`).
- Colours are 0..1 display colours. Units are metres, y is up, the camera looks down its local -Z. In 2D use x and y, keep physics bodies in one z plane, and put the drawing order in a `DrawLayer {layer}` (lowest first, the highest on top, whatever the z; none means layer 0; it orders sprites, tile maps, decals and particle clouds: `docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, "Draw order").
- **Attaching**: give an entity `Parent {target: <uid>}` and `LocalTransform` (position, rotation, scale relative to the parent) next to its `Transform`. After every tick and every edit its `Transform` is recomputed from the parent's, so it follows (a camera on a car, a weapon in a hand, a moon around a planet: spin the parent and the children travel). Chains of any depth work. Edit the `LocalTransform` of an attached entity, not its `Transform` (that one is overwritten). A parent that does not exist, or a cycle, leaves the entity where it is; `check` reports both. Deleting a parent does not delete its children.
- **Resources** are named global numbers (`score`, `time_left`, `input.space`): `set_resource`, `resources`. They are saved in scenes, hashed and replayed.

## Behaviour

Logic is data: `define_system` takes a JSON graph that runs every tick, deterministically, with no compiler. You can also write it as text: `define_script` takes Lunaris Script (`docs/SCRIPT.md`: `system gravity each Transform, Velocity { Velocity.lin[1] -= 9.8 * dt; }`), reports every mistake with a line and column, and `get_script` gives a defined system back as text to edit.

```json
{"name": "gravity", "each": ["Transform", "Velocity"], "do": [
  {"set": "Velocity.lin[1]", "to": {"sub": [{"get": "Velocity.lin[1]"}, {"mul": [9.8, {"dt": true}]}]}}]}
```

- Statements: `set`, `let`, `if` / `then` / `else`, `despawn`, `spawn`, `add`, `remove`, `res_set`, `res_add`, `emit`. Expressions: `get`, `var`, `res`, `dt`, `tick`, `uid`, `rand`, arithmetic and math (`add sub mul div mod neg abs sign sqrt floor ceil round trunc fract min max clamp lerp smoothstep noise sin cos tan atan atan2 exp ln pow hypot`), comparison, `and or not select`. Full reference: `docs/GRAPH.md`.
- `each` visits entities having all the listed components. `order` (default 100) sets which system runs first.
- **Input**: keys arrive as resources `input.<key>` (`w`, `a`, `space`, `arrowleft` ...), set with the `input` tool (`{"values": {"d": true}}`, a key stays pressed until you set it false). Read them with `{"res": "input.d"}`.
- **Touching**: `{"on_contact": {"a": ["Player"], "b": ["Orb"]}}` instead of `each` runs once per touching pair (needs `Collider`; mark pickups `trigger`). Fields are `a.Comp.field` / `b.Comp.field`, `{"despawn": "b"}`, `{"contact": "ny"}` (normal from a to b).
- **Speed**: `profile {ticks}` runs ticks and says which stage or graph system takes the time (`render: true` times a picture too).
- **Levels and menus**: `SceneSwitch {path}` on an entity, set `go` true from a graph system or button to load another scene file; `Persist` entities and the resources carry over (`docs/GAMEPLAY.md`).
- **Atmosphere**: `HeightFog {density, falloff, height, color}` adds valley fog to the GPU renderer, `Environment.fog` is the uniform kind (`docs/GPU.md`).
- **All the lighting effects at once**: `examples/lighting_showcase.lunaris.json` (eye adaptation, reflections, lens flare, probes: a courtyard next to a dark cellar; `lunaris play` it and walk with W A S D) is the one to copy (`docs/GPU.md`).
- **Dark rooms next to bright days**: an `AutoExposure {min, max, speed, target}` entity makes the GPU renderer (high qualities) adapt its exposure to how bright the picture is, over time in `lunaris play` and at once for a `render` (its reply says the exposure in `measured`); `Environment.exposure` stays the compensation on top (`docs/GPU.md`).
- **Mirrors and wet floors**: an `Ssr {strength, max_distance, thickness}` entity makes metallic or smooth (`roughness` below about 0.5) meshes and `Water` reflect what is on the screen in the GPU renderer's `high` and `ultra` qualities; the sky's reflection stays where nothing is found (`docs/GPU.md`).
- **Rooms and caves that reflect themselves**: a `ReflectionProbe {size, resolution}` entity (with a `Transform` in the middle of the room) captures the six views around it once and the surfaces inside its box reflect them and are lit by them instead of the sky (GPU high qualities, at most four probes); the `bake_probe {uid?}` tool captures it again after you moved things (`docs/GPU.md`).
- **Looking at the sun**: a `LensFlare {intensity, ghosts, halo}` entity draws a glare, ghosts and a halo from the first directional light when the sun is in view (GPU `high` and `ultra`); a mountain or a cube in front of the sun hides it (`docs/GPU.md`).
- **Screen fades and grading**: a `ColorGrade` entity (`fade [r,g,b,a]` for fade to black or a hit flash, `temperature`, `lift`/`gamma`/`gain`) in the GPU renderer (`docs/GPU.md`); animate it with a `Tween`.
- **Crowds**: `Steer` + `Velocity` gives seek/arrive, flee, wander and flocking by `group` (separation, alignment, cohesion), `bounds` fences and facing, with no graph system (`docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, `examples/flock.lunaris.json`).
- **First person**: `MouseLook` on a camera that is a child of a `CharacterBody` (`input.mouse_dx/dy` and the right stick turn it, `forward`/`right` steer the walk, set the resource `cursor.lock` to hide and hold the pointer; `docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, `examples/first_person.lunaris.json`).
- **Effects**: a `Particles` cloud plus `ParticleModules {modules}` (text: `color 0:... 1:...`, `size`, `wind`, `attract`, `vortex`, `noise`, `collide plane|sphere`, `trail`, `mesh cube`) gives fire, fountains that bounce, sparks with ribbons, tornadoes, debris (`docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, `examples/particle_fx.lunaris.json`).
- **2D animation**: `SpriteAnim {clips}` beside `Animator` + `Sprite`: `run: 4-9 fps=12 loop step@1`, one clip a line; the animator's clip chooses the picture, a `StateMachine` state `clip=run` switches it (`docs/GAMEPLAY.md`).
- **Controllers**: the player turns pads into `input.gamepad.a`, `.lx`, `.lt` ... (up and right positive, triggers 0..1; `gamepad2` ... for more pads); bind them in an `InputMap` (`move_x = d, a:-1, gamepad.lx`) and shake a pad with the resources `rumble.low` / `rumble.high` (`docs/GAMEPLAY.md`).
- **Levels**: `generate_map {uid, mode, seed}` makes a dungeon (`rooms`), cave, maze or winding cavern into a `Tilemap` from a seed, always connected, and says where to start, where the exit is and where to put enemies (`docs/GAMES.md`); `autotile` then draws the edges and `tilemap_colliders {uid, solid}` makes the map solid (merged static boxes, undoable; re-run after edits).
- **Shipping**: `export_game {out, name}` writes the world as a start scene plus every file it uses, the player and `play.bat` into a folder you can zip (it runs `check` first; `docs/EXPORT.md`).
- **Menus and HUD**: a `Ui` entity is a rectangle on the screen (`pos`/`size` as fractions of the picture, `color`, `fill` for bars, `text`, `border`, `layer`); with `button: true` it reacts to `input.mouse_x/y/left` and sets `Ui.clicked` for one tick, which a graph system turns into an effect. Test it by sending mouse input with the `input` tool (`docs/UI.md`; `examples/menu.lunaris.json`). Add a `UiStyle` for rounded corners, gradients, pictures and shadows, a `TextFont` for TrueType text (`wrap` breaks lines) and a `UiAnchor` to pin a name tag or health bar over a character in the world (`examples/ui_showcase.lunaris.json`). `Ui` sizes are fractions of both width and height, so boxes stretch on a screen that is not the shape you made them for: add one `UiScale {aspect: 1.7778}` entity and the interface is laid out in the biggest picture of that shape in the middle of any window (`UiEdge` pins single elements to the edges; `docs/UI.md`, "Other screens").
- **Long lists and richer widgets (UI kit v3, `docs/UI.md`, `examples/ui_kit3.lunaris.json`)**: a `UiScroll` on a `Ui` is a viewport: whatever has it as `Parent` (a `UiLayout` column taller than the viewport, rows, texts) is clipped to it and scrolls with `input.mouse_wheel` (set it with the `input` tool and back to 0), a drag, the focus moving with `ui_up`/`ui_down` or the right stick; a `UiScrollBar` on a button `Ui` is its scroll bar. `UiDropdown` (header button) + `UiOption` (each option) open a list and write the picked index to a resource, `UiTabs` + `UiTab` show one panel of several, and `UiGroup {shown}` hides a panel and everything under it (drawing, pointer, focus, layout): all as plain buttons you lay out and style yourself. `UiDrag` (on a button `Ui`, with an `item` number and the `slot` it sits in) and `UiDrop` (on each slot) make an inventory: the thing follows the pointer while held and is centred in the slot it is dropped on (trading places with what is there, or snapping back); a drop is reported for one tick in `UiDrop.dropped/from/item` and the resources `drop.NAME`, `drop.NAME.item`, `drop.NAME.count`. `UiTooltip {text, delay}` on a `Ui` plus one `UiTooltipBox` on a styled hidden `Ui` give tooltips (the engine fills the box and places it), and `UiRadial {value, start, clockwise, shape, inner}` on a `Ui` draws a cooldown wipe, ring or clock instead of a bar (set `value` from a resource in a graph system).
- **Walking somewhere**: give an entity a `CharacterBody` (or a `Velocity`) and a `NavAgent`, then `set NavAgent {target: [x, y, z], active: true}`: the engine finds the way around walls and up stairs and walks it there; `status` 2 means arrived, 3 no path. The `path` tool shows the route first (`docs/NAVIGATION.md`; `examples/maze.lunaris.json`).
- **Game blocks** (one component or tool each, with the text languages they read; `docs/GAMEPLAY.md`, `UI.md`, `AUDIO.md`, `PHYSICS.md`, `TERRAIN.md`): `InputMap` (named actions, rebinding), `StateMachine` (animation and AI states), `BehaviorTree`, `Tween`, `Timeline` (cutscenes), `CameraRig` (follow, orbit, shake), `Sensor`, `HitPoints`, `Spline` + `PathFollower`, `StreamZone` (big worlds), `Dialogue` (branching talk), `Ik` (reach and look), `AnimEvents` and `RootMotion`, `Particles` (up to 60,000 in one entity) and `EmitterFx`, `CameraView` (minimap, monitor, mirror), `Parallax`, `Terrain`, `MeshCollider` (a level from a `.glb`: stood on, rolled across, walked and navigated over, hit by rays) and `ConvexCollider` (a rock or crate with the shape of a model's convex hull), `Water`, `WaterBody` (rivers and lakes at different levels from a 16-bit picture of surface heights, 0 = dry; Tellus's `water.png` loads as it is: `heightmap`, `size`, `relief` metres for white; the engine puts `InWater {depth, surface, body}` on every `CharacterBody` whose feet are under it and takes it off when it climbs out, so a graph system `each ["CharacterBody", "InWater"]` runs only for waders; `Buoyant {density, drag}` makes a rigid body float; `water_depth {x, z, y?}` measures it; `docs/TERRAIN.md`, "Water bodies"; both together: `examples/terrain_layers.lunaris.json`), `NavObstacle`, `Joint` (ropes, hinges, ragdolls), `MusicPlayer` and `AudioMixer`, `UiStyle`, `TextFont` and `UiAnchor`, `Localization` (`{#key}` texts from `lang/*.json`; a `LanguageList` and the resource `language.index` switch the language from a graph system), `save_game`, and `noise(x, y, seed)` for procedural content. `schema` lists every component with its fields; each one's doc comment says what it does.
- **Prefabs**: anything that appears more than once is a prefab: `define_prefab` (components, children, inheritance), then `spawn_prefab` (with `at`, `overrides`, `count`) or `{"spawn_prefab": "Bullet", "at": [...], "velocity": [...]}` inside a graph system (`docs/PREFABS.md`). Scenes can hold `prefabs` and place `instances`.
- **Multiplayer**: a scene reads the first player's keys as `input.<key>` and the second's as `input.p2.<key>` (third `input.p3.*` ...); `lunaris host` relays and every copy of the game steps the same inputs (lockstep, or `--rollback` to play at once with guessed inputs and go back when a guess was wrong; a lobby, `lunaris watch` for spectators who replay the input log, a seat token to come back after a drop, `--sim-latency` to test a bad network: `docs/NETWORK.md`; `examples/duel.lunaris.json`). Anything random must use `{"rand": ...}`, and a rollback game must not change systems or prefabs while it runs: a drifting copy is reported as a desync with the tick.
- **Your own code**: `define_system` with `"wasm": "path/to/module.wasm"` (or a `.wat` text file) runs a sandboxed WebAssembly module over the entities (`fields`, `writes`, `res`, `fuel`): any language that compiles to WebAssembly, no permission needed, deterministic, bounded by a fuel budget (`docs/WASM.md`). Native `plugin` systems are faster but the person running the engine must allow them.
- **Reacting to events**: an `EventWatch` entity with `names: "coin, hit"` turns those events into resources `event.coin` (how many last tick) and `event.coin.x/.y/.z/.uid` (where and who), which any system can read.
- **Entities talking**: write a resource in one system (`res_set player.x`), read it in another (`{"res": "player.x"}`). A resource that was never set reads 0.
- **Sounds and messages**: `{"emit": "coin"}`; `step` and `events` return them (with the `pos` of the emitter); a `Sound` entity named `coin` decides how it sounds, a `SoundFile` makes it a WAV file placed in the world, an `AudioSource` plays by itself. The active camera is the listener, and sound is traced through the colliders: walls muffle it, rooms make it ring (`docs/AUDIO.md`).
- Camera follow, timers, animation (change `Sprite.frame` from `tick`) and win/lose banners (`Text.visible`) are all just systems; see `examples/orb_dash` and `examples/moon_hopper` (`get_system` shows each).

## Seeing

- `render {width, height, eye, target, fov, quality}` returns an image (and `png` bytes in the clients) plus `visible` entities with pixel counts and boxes. Without `eye` it uses the active Camera. `quality` is `reference` (the CPU renderer: identical on every machine, use it when a hash must match), or `classic`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `ultra` (the GPU renderer; the default is `high` when the host has a GPU). The reply says which renderer drew it. **To make a scene look good**: add an `Environment` entity (`sky`, `clouds`, `fog`, `bloom`, `ssao`, `exposure` ...) and give meshes materials (`metallic`, `roughness`, `texture` with one of `grass`, `dirt`, `stone`, `bricks`, `wood`, `sand`, `concrete`, `tiles`, `metal`, `uv_scale` to repeat it): the first directional light is the sun and casts shadows. `docs/GPU.md` has the whole list; `examples/vista.lunaris.json` shows it all. **Models and animation**: `files {kind: "model"}` lists the `.glb` / `.gltf` files, `model_info {path}` says how big a model is (bounds, so you can place and scale it) and, for a skinned model, its clips; give an entity `MeshRenderer {mesh: "models/x.glb"}` and `Animator {clip: "walk", speed: 1, looping: true}` and it plays (`time` scrubs, `speed` 0 freezes, the clock is simulation state so replays match). `examples/animation.lunaris.json` has five of them. `pick {x, y}` says which entity is at a pixel.
- `render {debug: ["colliders", "velocity", "targets", "lights", "sensors", "wheels"]}` (or `"all"`) draws what the simulation sees over the picture: the collider shapes (gray static, green dynamic, blue characters, yellow triggers), velocity arrows, where navigation agents are going, how far lights and sensors reach, the rays and wheels of vehicles. Lines are drawn in front of everything, one pixel wide.
- `minimap {around: uid, radius, cells}` is a character grid you can read in text: `@` is the entity, other things are the first letter of their name, up is -z.
- `raycast` (with `mask` or `layers` to look through some collision layers), `nearby {point, radius}`, `contacts`, `query {with, where}` answer "what is where".
- **Hearing**: `acoustics {listener, source}` says what a room does to sound (RT60, how enclosed it is, a verdict) and what reaches the listener from a source (how much gets through walls, the echoes with delay, level and direction). `record_audio {ticks, path}` runs the game and mixes it to a WAV, reporting the loudness in dB per ear, a timeline every quarter second, which sounds started, and `silent: true` when nothing was heard.

## Checking your work

- `check` first. Then `render` and look, `step` and watch `hash`, `resources` for the game state.
- Test a game by playing it: set `input` keys, `step` a few ticks at a time, read state, decide, repeat (`tools/play_orb_dash.py` is such a bot). Record the run; `replay` proves it reproduces without you.
- Or write the playtest as data and let `verify` run it on a scratch copy of the engine: `{"scene": ..., "steps": [{"input": {"d": true}}, {"step": 60}, {"expect": {"resource": "score", "ge": 1}}]}`. It reports each expectation with the value seen and the value wanted, never touches your world, and (deterministic) gives the same answer every time. Format: `docs/GAMES.md`.
- A graph system that fails at run time keeps the simulation going and records `last_error` (see `systems`); `check` reports it.

## Sharing a world

If a person edits the same world (the Lunaris Editor is a separate program with its own world for now), poll `revision` (cheap, read-only) to learn that someone else changed something: when the number differs from the last one you saw, `query` again before acting on old information. `undo` undoes the last edit, whoever made it, so use `undo_group` and take a `snapshot` before risky work.

## Pitfalls

- Plane and quad meshes are centred on the entity (a floor at y = 0 is at y = 0). Cubes and spheres are one unit across, centred.
- 3D colliders have depth: keep 2D bodies and the things they touch in the same z, or they never meet (the contact of two solid bodies at different z pushes them apart along z: give the drawing order to a `DrawLayer`, not to z; `check` warns about solid bodies at different z under an orthographic camera).
- A flat 2D follow camera: set the `CameraRig`'s `look_at` to false (the default tilts it a few degrees and sprites can then draw behind a big tilemap; `check` says so).
- `tilemap_colliders` gives its boxes the uids after the last entity: spawn your own entities by number above them, or pass `first_uid` (a number above everything you will spawn) so the boxes start where you say; a clash is refused with the name of the entity that holds the number (`duplicate_uid` says when it is one of the boxes).
- `Persist` entities and returning: an entity of the new scene with the number of a persistent one is skipped (the persistent one wins, `scene.skipped` counts), so a scene that holds the music can be switched back to; a `SceneReset {resources}` beside the `SceneSwitch` makes the named resources take the new scene's value; a menu that must take the focus when it appears needs `UiNavAuto` beside the `UiNav`.
- A whole game made of scenes, saves and an export is `examples/depths` (`docs/GAMES.md`); `docs/DOGFOOD.md` lists what was in the way.
- A text template placeholder is `{score}`; a resource nothing sets reads 0 forever (`check` warns).
- Events are output, not state: they are not in the hash, and a replay makes them again.
- A model shows one texture (PNG) and its rest pose; sprites and tile maps use nearest-neighbour sampling (crisp pixel art).
- `step` has a time budget (20 s by default); a long run returns `stopped_early` and you call it again.
- Files only exist inside the workspace folder; paths are relative and cannot use `..`.
