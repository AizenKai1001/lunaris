# The city as the world of a multiplayer game (`lunaris-citygen`, `lunaris server`)

The procedural city (docs/CITY.md) can be the map of a game with a server that is the truth and clients that predict. This is how the pieces fit; the game that does it keeps its own plan and log.

## Roles
`city/city.json` of the workspace: `{"seed": 1, "game": true, "plaza": [-1, 0, -1, 0], "server": {...}}`.
- **`Role::Server`** (`lunaris server`, or `"role": "server"`): no pictures, no furniture. Grids (`citygen grids ROOT SEED` prints their numbers as JSON for the scene builder): `s` the **collision shell** of each block, 260 m round every player (`shell.rs`: the walls with the street doorways open, a slab for every floor and the roof, about 1,800 triangles a block against 14,000 to 100,000 for what a player draws); `r` the **insides** of a building, two floors at a time, loaded only near it (`interior::server_floor`: the partitions with their doorways as a collision model, the interior doors, the things to search; the zone of the ground floors also carries the building's **street doors**, so that they are entities only while somebody is near the building). A server loads only `city/textures/materials.json` (the buildings are chosen from what the list has, so the file must be the same one the players have).
- **`Role::Client`** (`"game": true` on a player's program): the pictures and the colliders of the blocks, the streets' things and the far levels, but none of the entities a server owns (doors, containers). The `plaza` of blocks stays empty round the middle of the map for the game's own centrepiece.
- **`Role::Visual`**: the single-player city (`citygen city`), with its own doors and containers as entities of the city scene's own components.
Every avatar of a server carries a `StreamAnchor` (docs/GAMEPLAY.md), so the zones follow every player. A scene that has no `ProcGrid` does not bring the city up at all.

## What a server owns (`serverside.rs`)
Doors and containers as the engine's standard components, so that the server decides who opens what and the game's own systems (hit points, locks, loot) work on them: a door is `Door` + `Interactable` kind 1 + a box `Collider` + `NetEntity` (class 3), a container `Interactable` kind 2 + `Inventory` + `LootContainer` (the loot table is the one `city.json` names for the kind of room it stands in: `"tables": {"Kitchen": "city_kitchen", ...}`, `"refill"`) + `NetEntity`. `"server"."door"` and `"container"` add the game's own components (hit points, a team). The leaf of a door and the way it swings are the same geometry as the single-player door (`serverside::leaf`, a test for all orientations).
A still thing (`NetEntity` class 1 and 3) is told to the clients with its place, facing, flags and kind **only** (no `anim`, `anim_t`): the size of a door's leaf goes in the flags (bit 2 the swing sign, bits 3 to 8 the width and 9 to 14 the height in steps of 7.5 cm; bits 0 and 1 are the engine's open flags: `pack_flags`, `unpack_flags`), and the game's client system sets the puppet's scale and hinge from them.

## Cost, measured (200 swarm bots over a 1.5 km map, 40 s, real UDP on loopback, release build, i5-12600K)
The arena (500 entities): tick 7.2 ms, simulation 0.9 ms a step. The city, a uniform crowd so that every block, door and room is loaded all the time (about 8,000 entities): tick 39.8 ms at first, **21.8 ms** after `procgrid`, `streaming` and the character controller's grid were made to scale and `procgrid.every` was set to 4; simulation 11.5 ms a step down to 6.0 (`characters` 4.7 to 2.6, `procgrid` 2.8 to 0.4, `streaming` 0.7 to 0.3). With the 200 bots clustered in 300 x 300 m (a fight in one place): **11.3 ms** a tick, none of 1,200 over 33 ms. What is left of `characters` is the real work: capsules against the shells (an avatar is pushed out of a wall by the server: the defence against walking through one). `city_perf.py` in the game repository runs it.

**A sustained uniform crowd is the limit.** Those figures are before the whole map is loaded. 200 players that walk uniformly over 1.5 km keep loading blocks for about two minutes until about 13,000 entities exist (`LUNARIS_CENSUS=1 lunaris server --profile` prints what the world is made of), and the tick is then 50 to 60 ms against 6 to 7 ms for the arena with the same bots: the cost is about 2 microseconds an entity a step, spread over the systems (each gathers the world's solids for rays once a step: 4.3 ms for 13,000 solids, 2.2 ms since the grid became a flat counting-sort list; the street doors are loaded with the ground floor of their building and no longer by a block grid). A crowd in clumps is fine. Not done: one gathering of the static solids for all the systems, a long step for machines far from every player, doors and crates that become entities only when somebody is within a few metres.

## A round city and a baked one (2026-10-24, the game under a dome)
`city/city.json` may also say `"polar": true` (and `"dome": R`): the plan (`polar.rs`) is then rings and radii instead of a grid. Rings at 200, 310, 420, 530, 640 and 750 m (the last the boulevard along the wall), the eight avenues are the radii at multiples of 45 degrees, five bands of 16, 24, 32, 40 and 48 blocks, an apron band (5) out to the wall and the plaza of the Core (-1, 0). A block is named (band, sector) and is a quadrilateral (its tile, the centre lines of the four streets round it, less half a street on each side); lots are rectangles in strips along its four sides, each inside the quadrilateral and clear of the others (`Plan::polar_lots`); a park is laid out in the largest rectangle of the block's own frame (`Plan::local_rect`, `Plan::block_frame`); `Plan::blocks_in_cell` says which blocks a streaming cell holds (a ProcGrid asks `zones(grid, ci, cj)` by cell, a polar block is in the cell its tile's middle is in). The streets are laid by the tiles that own them (the ring road on a tile's inner side, the radial street on its first side, the crossing at its first corner), exactly as the rectilinear ground does, and are a little wider than the carriageway where two bands' chords do not meet. 53 tests, among them: lots stay in their block, clear of each other, the same every time; every block is in exactly one cell; the tiles cover the ring.
`citygen polar-export ROOT SEED` (export.rs) writes that plan as JSON: the material library, every block (tile, quad, frame, land use, park) and every lot with the building the engine decided on it (volumes, floors, heights, how the walls are made, roofs, spires and its doors). `citygen shellprobe` and the other probes read the workspace's `city/city.json` for the plan.
**Baked mode** (`City::with_baked`, on by default when `city/baked/` exists; `LUNARIS_CITY_BAKED=0` turns it off): `b0_I_J.glb` (the level-0 model of block (I, J)), `b1_I_J.glb` (level 1) and `c2_CI_CJ.glb` (a far cell) in that folder are served instead of made; every coordinate in a file is relative to the block's middle (the cell's middle for a far cell). A modelling program made them (the game's Blender kit, `art/city/blender/` in the game's folder), standing on the same footprints and opening at the same doors as the buildings the engine decides on: the engine still makes the collision shells, the insides, the street doors and the crates from the same specs. A baked block is collided with by its shell (`s` item, a child of the block's prefab and an asset of its zone), so a client and the server collide with the same walls; the level-1 trees stay the engine's (`Item::Cards`). A block without a baked file is made as before, so a city can be baked in part.

## Tests
`cargo test -p lunaris-citygen` (47: a ray stops at a shell's wall and passes its doorway, a tower's shell has a slab for every floor, the server's floor has the doors of the drawn floor, a door swings into the building whichever way its wall faces, the server's rooms have partitions, doors and containers); the game's live test `city_smoke.py` runs a server and two clients on the real binaries (a wall stops bullets, a shut door stops them, the use key opens the door and the doorway lets them through, the server loads the inside of the building a player stands in and a crate there opens).
